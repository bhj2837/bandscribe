AREA: direct-models

[SUMMARY]
Your instinct is only partly right. As of Sept 2026, role-based lead/rhythm guitar separation does exist as a product, but no one has published a verifiable benchmark for it, and there are no open weights. There are two direct offerings. (1) MVSEP's "MVSep Lead/Rhythm Guitar (lead-guitar, rhythm-guitar)", sep_type 101 in its API, offers a two-stage model (SDR 9.21, the default) and a one-stage model (SDR 9.02). It was announced in MVSEP news on 2026-03-21, and its demo file is dated 2025-11. (2) Moises launched "Lead & Rhythm" and "Acoustic & Electric" guitar splits on 2024-04-24, with no quality numbers. MVSEP's 9.21 SDR comes from an undisclosed internal validation set. There is no public lead/rhythm leaderboard, and neither vendor defines exactly what counts as "lead", so the quality claims are vendor-reported, not independently checked. Every other service I checked splits guitar only by instrument type, never by role: LALAL.AI (Electric/Acoustic, plus a combined Guitars stem from Aug 2026 that explicitly "doesn't tell guitar types apart"), AudioShake (guitar / guitar_electric / guitar_acoustic), Fadr Plus (Acoustic/Electric), and MVSEP's own Acoustic and Electric Guitar models plus its Mega 53-stem model. iZotope RX Music Rebalance has no guitar stem at all. The open ecosystem has nothing for this: ZFTurbo's MSST public pretrained list and the UVR / python-audio-separator lists contain only htdemucs_6s for guitar. Academic work confirms that same-timbre separation is still weak. In GuitarDuets (ISMIR 2024), the second guitar in a classical duet reaches only about 1 dB SDR on real recordings. Shibata et al. (ICASSP 2019) jointly transcribed lead, bass and rhythm guitar and got only about 24% F-measure on lead. Role-based separation of a single source class can clearly be learned, though. MVSEP's lead/back-vocal model (10.41/6.61 SDR) and its SATB choir model (6.6–8.7 SDR) are working analogues. Spatial cues are the strongest non-learned lever. SpaIn-Net shows that conditioning on panning helps pull apart two guitar tracks (+1.8 dB SI-SDR, simulated), and MVSEP hosts learned "phantom centre" extractors (up to 18.93 dB centre SDR). This fits scenario A (hard-panned double-tracked rhythm plus a centred lead) well. By scenario: D (acoustic rhythm + electric lead) is essentially solved by acoustic/electric splitters. A is very tractable with guitar-stem extraction plus a centre/sides split. C (clean rhythm + overdriven lead) is plausible because MoisesDB's taxonomy separates clean from distorted electric, but I found no public clean/distorted separator. B (same tone, both centred) and F (mono/live) are where results will be weakest. Practical verdict: don't train anything first. Send a few representative songs through MVSEP sep_type 101 (two-stage) and, optionally, a Moises Pro trial. For panned mixes, add a cheap local step that splits the guitar stem into centre and sides. For the hard cases, fall back to the symbolic side after Basic Pitch: take the monophonic upper line during detected solo sections as lead, and treat the rest as chords/strumming for rhythm. Accept that B and F will need manual correction in the tab editor.

[RECOMMENDATIONS]
 - Start with MVSEP sep_type 101 using the default two-stage model. Run it through the API on 5–10 songs you know well, covering scenarios A, B, C, E and F, and listen critically. It is the only dedicated lead/rhythm model with any published number. Treat the SDR 9.21 as vendor-reported only.
 - If you want a second opinion on the same songs, try a Moises Pro trial and compare the lead/rhythm stems. No independent quality data exists for either service, so your own listening test is the real benchmark.
 - Add a cheap local spatial step for scenario A, where the rhythm is hard-panned and the lead is centred. First extract a guitar stem (BS Roformer SW in UVR 5.6 or on MVSEP). Then split centre from sides with a per-bin pan-ratio mask or MVSEP's Phantom Centre model. Compare the result with MVSEP 101 and keep whichever transcribes more cleanly in Basic Pitch.
 - Route scenario D (acoustic rhythm + electric lead) to an acoustic/electric splitter (MVSep 66/81, AudioShake or LALAL.AI) instead of a role splitter. That problem is essentially solved.
 - For B and F, don't expect clean audio separation. Do the split on the symbolic side after Basic Pitch: gate solo sections with a guitar-solo detector, take the top monophonic line there as lead, and handle the rest as chords plus a strumming pattern in the style of Lukoianov & Klapuri. Plan for manual fixes in the tab editor.
 - Build a small personal evaluation set before investing further. Mix individual guitar tracks from MoisesDB (clean, distorted and acoustic are labelled; non-commercial use is allowed) into synthetic A/B/C/D mixtures, so you can measure SDR or transcription F1 per scenario rather than rely on vendor numbers.
 - Don't train your own lead/rhythm separator on the RTX 2060. Academic results on same-timbre separation are poor (about 1 dB for the second source), and a service model already exists. Only consider cloud training if MVSEP 101 fails badly on the scenarios you care about most.

[VERIFIED TAKEAWAYS]
 - Only two dedicated lead/rhythm guitar separators exist, both cloud-only. MVSEP sep_type 101 has vendor-reported SDR 9.21 (two-stage) / 9.02 (one-stage) on an undisclosed set; no public leaderboard, no description. Moises Lead & Rhythm is Pro-only with no metrics. Neither can be benchmarked independently, so your own listening and transcription tests are the real evaluation.
 - No open weights exist for lead/rhythm, and none for acoustic/electric or clean/distorted either. I checked the MSST pretrained list, UVR's download list, python-audio-separator's model list and a Hugging Face 'guitar' search. Open weights for extracting a single combined guitar stem do exist, and they are good: BS-Roformer-SW scores 9.01 on MVSEP's public guitar set, becruily's Mel-RoFormer 5.3–5.6.
 - Stage 1 (full mix to guitar stem) can therefore run locally with BS-Roformer-SW via python-audio-separator. Only the role split (stage 2) needs MVSEP 101 or Moises. VRAM use on a 6 GB RTX 2060 is unverified.
 - The one cross-vendor number available is for plain guitar stems, not lead/rhythm: Moises's guitar stem (6.94) trails the open BS-Roformer-SW (9.01) on MVSEP's guitar set. There is no evidence that Moises's lead/rhythm split beats MVSEP's.
 - Academic evidence on separating same-timbre guitars is weak. In GuitarDuets on real recordings, the second guitar scores 0.2–1.4 dB SDR with negative SI-SDR, and predicted notes do not help it meaningfully. No 2025–2026 paper on separating lead from rhythm guitar in audio was found.
 - The symbolic fallback is established but modest. Shibata 2019 gets lead F 24.1%, and its rhythm chord accuracy of 74.8% equals the Madmom baseline. An open implementation of the heuristic already exists in TabForge (chords go to rhythm, upper-register runs go to lead, plus top-note peeling).
 - Solo gating for scenario E is well supported. The released segment-level guitar-solo classifier reports 87.5 guitar F1 on a balanced test set built from live recordings with bleed.
 - Corrections to carry forward: the MVSEP demo list is user uploads; AudioShake's guitar models cost 1.0 credit/min, not up to 1.5; Moises's free-tier guitar stem is unverified; scenario D is promising but not proven solved (no acoustic/electric metrics exist); the Mid/Side assumption that double-tracked rhythm is uncorrelated and the lead is dry and centred is only an approximation.

AREA: spatial

[SUMMARY]
Stereo position is the cheapest usable cue for telling lead from rhythm guitar, because the usual rock/metal mix puts two separately played rhythm takes hard left and right and the solo in the middle. But it only tells you where a guitar sits in the stereo field, not what role it plays. Existing tools say so themselves. Stemslayer, a Windows app first published in August 2026, runs htdemucs_6s and then splits the guitar stem into a mid lane and a side lane, and its README states outright that it "does not separate lead guitar from rhythm guitar". It also keeps its own lead/rhythm profile switched off because no public model qualified. The plain mid/side split has a problem people often miss: a guitar panned hard to one side lands equally in the mid and side channels. So the 'centre' lane still holds both rhythm takes at the same level as the lead. What you do get is a side channel (L-R) that contains no dry lead at all, as long as the lead is a mono signal panned dead centre. The classic masking methods are ADRess (Barry et al., AES 2004), the Avendano-Jot panning index and coherence measure, DUET, and PROJET, which is implemented in nussl. They split sound by level and phase differences between channels in each time-frequency bin, and they assume sources do not overlap much there. Dense, same-note, double-tracked distortion breaks that assumption. I ran a synthetic test that models a clean guitar stem in scenario A: two independent hard-panned rhythm takes plus a centred lead at 0 dB relative to each take. The best purely spatial audio masks raised the lead-to-rhythm ratio from 0 dB to only about 6 dB, against 15 dB for an oracle mask. The same test also covered two alternatives. At the pitch level, subtracting the side channel's pitch salience from the mid channel's raised lead pitch accuracy from 60% to 99%. With a single rhythm take panned 30% left, a phase-and-level mask worked almost perfectly (99%). The spatial methods failed in four cases: a rhythm guitar in the centre (0%), a lead 6 to 12 dB quieter than the rhythm, a fake double made by delaying one take, and quad-tracking. Separators seem to keep panning intact: htdemucs_ft changed the level balance of the stereo MUSDB 'other' stem by a median of only 0.11 dB. Spatially conditioned separation has been done: SpaIn-Net (ICASSP 2022) took two same-class guitars from negative SIR to about 4.5-4.8 dB. But it was trained on synthetic Slakh mixes with random panning, and no public checkpoint exists for real lead/rhythm material. One practical trap: Basic Pitch loads audio as mono at 22,050 Hz, so any spatial processing has to happen before transcription. Verdict: spatial processing should be the first stage for scenario A, and for C/D whenever the parts are panned apart. The best use is to take the side channel (or the L/R channels) as a clean rhythm reference and do the lead/rhythm split at the note level: notes in the mid channel minus notes in the side channel. Pure audio masking only gives a modest gain here. Decide per section which method to use, based on coherence and side-to-mid energy, and fall back to timbre, register, or role rules for B and F (centred or mono), where stereo gives nothing.

[RECOMMENDATIONS]
 - Keep the guitar stem in stereo at 44.1 kHz and never feed it straight to Basic Pitch, which downmixes to mono at 22,050 Hz and hands you M = lead + both rhythms. Write M, S and any masked signals as separate mono WAVs and transcribe each one.
 - Algorithm sketch for scenario A, step 1: get the stereo guitar stem (a BS-RoFormer/MVSep guitar model, or htdemucs_6s as the free local option). STFT at 44.1 kHz with n_fft 4096 and hop 1024, then compute M = (L+R)/2 and S = (L-R)/2.
 - Step 2 (spatial diagnosis per 2-4 bar segment): compute (a) the side/mid energy ratio, (b) broadband inter-channel coherence |<L R*>|/sqrt(<|L|^2><|R|^2>) with about 100-200 ms smoothing, and (c) the ILD histogram. Read them as follows. High side energy with low coherence means uncorrelated wide doubles (A): go to step 3. High coherence with a clear off-centre ILD peak means single panned takes (B2, C, D): use an IPD/ILD or ADRess mask at that azimuth. S near zero means mono or centred (B, F): skip spatial processing and fall back to timbre, register and role rules. High coherence with comb-like S means a fake double: treat it as B. The thresholds are not calibrated and need tuning on 10-20 known songs.
 - Step 3 (A): rhythm = S, since it holds no dry centred lead. If the takes are tight enough that S sounds thin or comb-filtered, also transcribe L and R and take the intersection. Transcribe S → rhythm notes. Transcribe M, and optionally M times the IPD/ILD mask → candidate notes. Lead notes = candidates with no matching note in S (same pitch within ±1 semitone, onsets within ±50 ms), or whose M/S salience ratio is above about 6 dB. Then apply a monophonic lead constraint (Viterbi over the M-minus-S salience), a register prior, and pitch-bend/vibrato cues from Basic Pitch.
 - Step 4: segment lead activity (scenario E) from the frame-wise energy of M's residual after the rhythm is explained, i.e. P_M - P_S smoothed over about 1 s. Use it to switch between 'rhythm only' and 'lead + rhythm' sections, so the rhythm transcription is not polluted where the lead is absent.
 - Build a small evaluation set before tuning: MoisesDB songs that have several distorted-guitar tracks (re-label lead/rhythm by hand; non-commercial licence is fine for personal use), plus a few of your own DI re-amped double-tracks. Score note-level F1 per role, not SDR, because the goal is tabs.
 - Treat stereo as the first stage and not the whole solution. For B, F and quiet leads, add a second, non-spatial cue: a role classifier on note sequences (register, polyphony, rhythmic density, bends), or later a trained model. When you have GPU budget, generate synthetic role-labelled mixes with realistic panning templates (hard-L/R uncorrelated doubles, quad-tracking, a lead centred or off-centre with stereo FX, mono and live variants) and fine-tune a guitar separator in MSST. Pan-angle conditioning (SpaIn-Net style) helped by about 2.8 dB on same-class guitars in the literature.
 - Before writing code, use Moises 'Lead & Rhythm Guitar' as a paid baseline on the same test songs to see whether the in-house pipeline actually adds anything.

[VERIFIED TAKEAWAYS]
 - A public lead/rhythm guitar separator already exists: MVSep Lead/Rhythm Guitar, since 2025-11-20, with self-reported SDR 9.21 (two-stage) and 9.02 (one-stage) on an undisclosed set. Moises (2024-04-24) and Music.AI 'Guitar Parts' also exist but publish no metrics. The research's claim that MVSep had none is refuted. Benchmark these on your own A-F test songs before building a spatial pipeline.
 - The physics of stereo splitting is verified. A hard-panned guitar puts equal energy into M=(L+R)/2 and S=(L-R)/2, so a plain mid/side split leaves both rhythm takes in the 'centre' lane at the same level as the lead. S excludes a lead only when the lead is exactly mono and centred (Avendano & Jot 2002 state this for L-R).
 - The synthetic scenario-A numbers reproduce exactly when re-run: best spatial audio mask (IPD/ILD) about 6 dB SIR, against 15.3 dB for the oracle. They come from one idealized 8-second clip with no separator bleed, so treat them as upper-bound sanity checks.
 - The '99% lead pitch' result for mid-minus-side is frame-level predominant-f0 argmax on synthetic audio, not Basic Pitch note transcription. The 'single rhythm 30% left: IPD/ILD 99%' and 'centred rhythm 0.4%' cases were run with the lead 6 dB below the rhythm, which the research did not say. Real-song note-level evidence is missing.
 - Basic Pitch really does downmix to mono at 22,050 Hz (inference.py line 239). Any stereo-based split must write separate mono files (M, S, masked) before transcription.
 - Evidence that separators preserve stereo is weaker than claimed. The ISMIR 2025 ∆ILD of 0.11 dB is a whole-signal broadband energy ratio on MUSDB 'other', not per-bin cues, and the 2026 follow-up finds ITD metrics unreliable. Whether BS-RoFormer/MVSep guitar stems keep per-bin IPD/ILD is untested.
 - Mixing guides agree that double- or quad-tracked rhythm is panned hard L/R (Nail The Mix, musicguymixing, Neural DSP, Wikipedia). Lead placement is less standard: centred 'often', or 10-20% off-centre when a vocal is present (Nail The Mix). Neural DSP does NOT prescribe a centred solo.
 - SpaIn-Net's gain for two same-class guitars (SIR -2.3/-3.2 to 4.8/4.5 dB, about +2.8 dB) is confirmed but comes only from synthetic Slakh with constant-power panning. Its code is public (MIT) with no weights.
 - For evaluation data, MoisesDB has no role labels (types: clean/distorted electric, acoustic, lap steel/slide; CC BY-NC-SA 4.0). MedleyDB's stem-level 'component: melody' flag gives about 18-30 songs where a melody guitar stem sits next to other guitar stems, the quickest route to a small real lead-vs-rhythm test set.
 - norbert is a real MIT multichannel Wiener filter, but released HTDemucs does not use Wiener post-filtering (wiener_iters=0, cac=True). nussl (last release 2021) and norbert (last release 2019) are unmaintained and may need pinned dependencies.
 - Scenario D (acoustic rhythm + electric lead) does not need stereo cues at all. MVSep has separate Acoustic Guitar and Electric Guitar models (2025), and Moises has acoustic/electric separation.

AREA: symbolic-role

[SUMMARY]
Splitting lead from rhythm after transcription is possible, but the research behind it is thin. The only published direct attack is Régnier, Martin and Bigo (ISMIR 2021). It labels whole bars of a clean, single-part Guitar Pro tab as rhythm or not, using 31 hand-made features and an LSTM, and reaches F1 0.95. Checking only whether a bar contains chords or single notes already gives F1 0.87. It does not split notes that two guitars play at the same time. It fails on arpeggiated solos, on chord bursts inside solos and on short rhythm fills, and its predictions break into more, shorter sections than the reference labels. The closest note-level method is Dong et al.'s "Arranger" (ISMIR 2021), a BiLSTM that labels each note of a symbolic mixture by part. It reaches 77% accuracy on pop and 97% on Bach, but only 74% and 53% where two parts use the same instrument (two violins, two NES pulse channels). Two distorted guitars are exactly that case, so same-timbre, same-texture guitars (twin leads, doubled riffs) are the fundamental limit. Transcription quality is the second limit. On the GOAT set of isolated, distorted DI electric guitar, a September 2026 paper (Harmonica) reports note F1 of 0.553 for Basic Pitch, 0.680 for YourMT3+ and 0.750 for its own largest model. No public benchmark I found covers two electric guitars sounding together. Real input would be a separated stem with two guitars, so expect clearly worse results, but that number is unmeasured. Some tools help. YourMT3+/MT3-style models have separate acoustic, clean-electric and distorted-electric guitar classes, which covers scenarios C and D at the class level. Basic Pitch outputs a pitch-bend curve for each note, a strong lead cue, but only keeps bends on overlapping notes when multiple_pitch_bends=True. For time segmentation (scenario E) there are ready tools: a MERT-based lead-instrument detector (ICASSP 2025, MIT code and checkpoints, segment-level guitar-solo F1 87.5 vs 20.8 for an older SVM) and the all-in-one structure analyzer (MIT, outputs 'solo' and 'inst' labels). Classic melody tools (skyline highest-note rule, Melodia, pitch-proximity voice separation) are weak baselines: skyline gets only 56.6% melody recall on POP909. Verdict: symbolic separation is a useful second stage, not a complete answer. The best fit is a hybrid. Use stereo placement for scenario A and instrument class for C and D. Use solo detection to label time sections for E. For B and F, assign each note by rules or a trained model using chord membership, register, bends/vibrato, duration and pan. Keep a person correcting the result for twin leads and doubled riffs, which no method found here can separate.

[RECOMMENDATIONS]
 - Use symbolic role assignment as the second stage of a hybrid pipeline, not as the only separator. For scenario A (double-tracked hard L/R rhythm plus centered lead), first split by stereo placement (mid vs side) and transcribe each part separately. Stereo is far more reliable there than any symbolic rule, and symbolic reasoning is only needed to clean up leaked notes.
 - For transcription of distorted guitar, prefer YourMT3+ (GOAT note F1 0.680) over Basic Pitch (0.553). If you use Basic Pitch for its pitch-bend output, set multiple_pitch_bends=True; otherwise bends on overlapping notes are lost, and bends are one of the strongest lead cues.
 - Start with a transparent per-note rule scorer before any ML. Features: chord membership (2+ simultaneous notes), register relative to the local median, bend depth and vibrato, duration, pan ratio, and whether the pattern repeats bar to bar. Smooth labels over time with an HMM or a minimum segment length, because the Régnier model's main failure was isolated one-bar flips.
 - Get a free lead/rhythm prior from time segmentation. Run the MERT lead-instrument detector (MIT, checkpoints) or all-in-one 'solo' labels. Outside detected solo/lead spans, default guitar notes to rhythm. Inside solo spans, still run the note-level split, because rhythm guitar usually continues under the solo in two-guitar bands.
 - If rules plateau, train an Arranger-style BiLSTM note-to-part classifier on DadaGP songs with two guitar tracks (request access). Label track roles automatically with Régnier-style bar features, and train on noisy input (drop about 20-30% of notes, add octave errors and timing jitter) so it matches real transcriptions. This fits easily on the RTX 2060.
 - Use instrument classes where they exist. YourMT3+/MuScriptor tokenizers have separate acoustic, clean-electric and distorted-electric guitar classes, which handles scenarios C and D almost for free. Check the class outputs on a few known songs first, because MT3-style models show instrument leakage.
 - Build a small personal evaluation set before optimizing. Use songs where you know the parts (official tabs, or GP files you can render through an amp sim to make synthetic two-guitar mixes). Measure note-level role accuracy separately for scenarios A-F, since no public benchmark exists.
 - Design the output for human correction. Export two tabs (rhythm, lead) with per-note confidence, and a third 'ambiguous' track for notes the classifier cannot place (doubled riffs, twin harmonized leads). Accept that twin leads and unison riffs will need manual assignment or a pan-based rule.

[VERIFIED TAKEAWAYS]
 - The only direct symbolic rhythm-vs-lead work is still Régnier et al. 2021: bar-level labels, one guitar track at a time, F1 0.95 on clean ground-truth tabs, never tested on audio transcriptions. Its numbers are accurately reported.
 - No released model or benchmark separates two SIMULTANEOUS guitars by role, in either the audio or the symbolic domain. As of 2026-09-24, MVSep lists no lead/rhythm guitar model.
 - Arranger's weak string-quartet and game-music scores are dataset-level accuracies with no timbre input. They support 'interchangeable parts are hard', not a measured limit for two distorted guitars. Its pitch hints (about +17 points on game music) support letting the user tag a few lead notes.
 - For distorted guitar transcription, released-checkpoint evidence favours MuScriptor-medium (GOAT OnP F1 0.710) over Basic Pitch (0.553). The often-quoted YourMT3+ 0.680 comes from a model retrained on GOAT. Clean vs distorted vs acoustic class accuracy has never been reported for any model.
 - Basic Pitch's Python note_events keep per-note pitch-bend curves whatever multiple_pitch_bends is set to (the flag only changes the MIDI export), so bend/vibrato features are cheap to get.
 - Duan-Han-Pardo streaming assumes monophonic sources and forbids simultaneous pitches from sharing a source, so it cannot be applied as-is to a chordal rhythm guitar.
 - For scenario A, mid/side is not enough. The side channel removes a centered lead but the mid channel still contains both hard-panned rhythm guitars. Use azimuth-based separation (ADRess-style) or treat the side channel as rhythm-only.
 - A promising 2025 alternative for the rhythm part is to transcribe it as a strum pattern plus chords rather than notes. Lukoianov & Klapuri got strum F1 of about 96-97 from mixes containing other guitar parts, but the data and weights are proprietary.
 - Solo-segment detection is usable off the shelf (MIT checkpoint, mixture input), but its 87.5 guitar F1 comes from 2 internal live performances. Validate it on your own songs.
 - Tab back ends: Noise2Fret ships code but no weights, and TART and Harmonica have no public code. Harmonica is usable only through BandLab's Audio-to-MIDI service.

AREA: guided-informed

[SUMMARY]
Lead and rhythm guitar differ by role, not instrument class. Every conditioning signal that works at the class level therefore breaks down for scenario B/F, where both guitars have similar tone and sit in the same place. Banquet, the open MoisesDB query-by-example separator (MIT, 24.9M params, runs on an RTX 2060), turns the query into a PaSST embedding averaged over time. Its authors report that a query taken from the same song scores within 0.2 dB of one from a different song, and that the model is "insensitive to fine differences in timbre between different renditions". So a clip of the solo used as the query will not tell lead from rhythm. It can at most separate by timbre class (clean vs distorted vs acoustic), which only helps in scenarios C and D, and even there fine-class SNRs are around 0-1 dB. Meta SAM Audio (released 2025-12-16; small 500M / base 1B / large 3B; gated weights; SAM License; Python>=3.11) does exist and supports span prompting with '+' (present) and '-' (absent) time anchors. However, span tokens are only frame-level active/silent flags. The paper itself says span prompting is ambiguous when several events overlap, and Meta says separating highly similar events remains a challenge. It also cannot take an audio clip as a prompt. At best it is a scenario-E tool: '+' on solo sections and '-' on rhythm-only verses. The official VRAM is far above 6 GB, and only a third-party 'lite' repack reports 2.84 GB for base (bf16, tested on an RTX 4090). The strongest guided signal for same-timbre parts is note content (score or melody). GuideSep (ISMIR 2025, MIT, checkpoint on HF) conditions a 93.3M-param 16 kHz diffusion separator on a monophonic 'mimicry' melody plus an optional painted mel mask. It was trained on mimicry rendered from MIDI with FluidSynth, so a rendered first-pass lead transcription fits that training setup exactly. Lead guitar is mostly monophonic, which suits it, while rhythm would be the residual. Score-informed NMF (Ewert and Muller; libfmp code) was built exactly for separating note groups of one instrument (piano left/right hand, about 11.3 dB SDR on synthetic data). The GuitarDuets study on same-timbre guitar duets gives the catch: ground-truth notes help a lot, while automatically predicted notes help only marginally. A user-supplied Guitar Pro tab is therefore worth far more than a Basic Pitch first pass. Interactive commercial tools confirm the picture. The SpectraLayers 12 'Unmix Instrument' (1-10 s solo reference) is reported by users not to work for lead/rhythm, and both the SpectraLayers developer and RipX staff recommend panning filters plus manual note or harmonic selection. Practical verdict: because the end goal is tabs, not stems, split the parts in the symbolic domain. Transcribe the whole guitar stem, assign notes to lead or rhythm using stereo position (scenario A), time segmentation (E) and monophony/register/chord heuristics with a user-correction UI, or align to a user-provided tab. Use audio-domain guided separation (pan masking, GuideSep with a rendered lead melody, score-informed NMF masks) only as an optional refinement step to re-transcribe a cleaner lead. Test SAM Audio and the commercial Moises/MVSEP lead/rhythm models as baselines, not as the core.

[RECOMMENDATIONS]
 - Treat lead/rhythm as a symbolic-domain assignment problem first. Stem-separate the guitar stem once (stereo preserved), transcribe it, then label notes as lead or rhythm. Tabs need note ownership, not a pristine lead stem, so audio-domain separation is optional refinement.
 - Always exploit stereo before any AI. Compute a pan-index histogram of the guitar stem. If there is clear L/R plus center energy (scenario A), apply pan-index masking to get a center (lead) and a sides (rhythm) signal, and use per-note pan position as the strongest labeling cue. This is cheap, CPU-only and covers the most common rock/metal mix.
 - Segment the song in time (scenario E). In sections where the guitar stem is harmonically chordal or rhythmic with no sustained monophonic line above, assign everything to rhythm. Only overlapping solo or fill regions need real separation. Let the user correct segment boundaries in the UI.
 - If the user has a Guitar Pro tab, use it. Render it to audio, align with chroma-DTW, label notes by track (lead/rhythm), then either transfer labels to the transcription or build score-informed NMF masks with libfmp. GuitarDuets shows ground-truth notes help far more than estimated ones.
 - For same-timbre, centered cases (B/F) where a cleaner lead is needed for accurate bends and vibrato: extract the monophonic top line from the first-pass transcription, render it with FluidSynth (this matches GuideSep's training setup), run GuideSep on the guitar stem (optionally with a painted mask on the solo region), then re-run transcription on the extracted lead. Iterate at most 1-2 times, since estimated-note conditioning gives diminishing returns.
 - Do not rely on query-by-example (Banquet, SpectraLayers Unmix Instrument) to tell lead from rhythm. Use it only when the two parts differ in timbre class (C: clean vs distorted; D: acoustic vs electric).
 - Evaluate SAM Audio cheaply before committing. In a separate Python 3.11 venv, try base with sam-audio-kit Lite Mode in fp16 on the RTX 2060, or rent a 24 GB cloud GPU for an hour. Test text 'electric guitar solo' with '+' anchors on the solo and '-' anchors on rhythm-only verses, on 5-10 songs. Keep it as a fallback, not the backbone.
 - Build a small personal evaluation set of songs with official multitracks or isolated guitar tracks, labeled lead/rhythm. Run Moises and MVSEP lead/rhythm on the same songs as a commercial baseline, and score by downstream note F1 of the lead part, not SDR.
 - Design the app as user-in-the-loop from the start: waveform or pianoroll view with lead/rhythm note coloring, click-to-reassign, draggable solo-section spans, and an optional 'play the lead into the mic' input for GuideSep-style mimicry. All the evidence (RipX, SpectraLayers, ISSE) says some manual correction is needed for similar-tone parts.

[VERIFIED TAKEAWAYS]
 - No published, verifiable benchmark exists for automatic lead-vs-rhythm guitar separation, academic or commercial. Moises (2024-04-24) and MVSEP (in use by Nov 2025, announced 2026-03-21) publish no metrics. Treat them as baselines to test on your own songs.
 - Query/timbre conditioning cannot split by role. Banquet's authors say it is insensitive to fine timbre differences. Its real fine-class guitar medians are about 0.4-1.1 dB (clean electric), 1.7-1.8 dB (acoustic) and 2.4-3.0 dB (distorted), not the '0-1 dB' in the research, which quoted lower quartiles. QSCNet raises these to 1.3 / 3.6 / 4.0 dB, still at class level.
 - Stereo position is the cheapest strong cue for scenario A. SpaIn-Net showed pan-angle conditioning can disentangle two same-class guitar tracks (+1.8 dB SI-SDR on simulated Slakh). Classic pan-index/ADRess/PROJET masking is available off the shelf (nussl, RipX Stereo Pass, M/S plugins, MVSEP Phantom Centre). None of it helps scenario F.
 - Note content is the strongest cue when timbre and pan are shared. Score-informed NMF split same-instrument note groups at 11.3 dB (+1.2 dB with onsets) on synthetic piano and tolerates about 200 ms misalignment. In GuitarDuets, ground-truth notes raised two same-timbre guitars from about 2.6 to 4.7-4.9 dB, but estimated notes were mixed and even hurt guitar 2 (1.98 vs 2.57 dB). A user-supplied tab is worth far more than a Basic Pitch first pass.
 - GuideSep's 10.46 dB relies on masks simulated from ground truth (mimicry-only is 9.60 dB; guitar class 9.96-10.53 dB). It was tested only on synthetic Slakh, and the authors warn about polyphonic targets and parts that share a melody. Using a rendered lead line as mimicry is reasonable but unproven on real distorted guitar.
 - SAM Audio: it exists, is gated, uses the SAM License and needs Python >= 3.11. It has no audio-reference prompts, and span prompting is admitted to be ambiguous for overlapping events. There are no official VRAM figures. The third-party Lite mode reports 2.84 GB for base in bf16 on a 4090, and float16 is listed as supported but not benchmarked, so an RTX 2060 needs a test. It is at best a scenario-E helper.
 - There is no public dataset with lead/rhythm role-labeled guitar stems. MoisesDB labels only acoustic, clean electric and distorted electric. Usable substitutes for synthesizing and evaluating role separation: GuitarSet comp/solo pairs (CC BY 4.0), MedleyDB's 'component: melody' stem field, and DadaGP/SynthTab (two distorted guitars, 'often one rhythmic and one lead').
 - Automatic time segmentation for scenario E has a ready starting point in guitar solo detection (Pati & Lerch 2017, 60 annotated rock songs). For the rhythm part's tab, strum-pattern transcription (Lukoianov & Klapuri, WASPAA 2025) gives a role-specific alternative to note-level separation.
 - The research's overall verdict still holds but remains unbenchmarked: split lead from rhythm in the symbolic domain with a user-correction UI, use stereo and time cues first, and use guided audio separation only as refinement.

AREA: train-your-own

[SUMMARY]
No public dataset labels guitar parts as lead or rhythm. MoisesDB (240 songs, 14.4 h, non-commercial) splits guitar only into acoustic, clean electric and distorted electric. MedleyDB uses the same kind of instrument labels plus a stem-level 'component' tag ('melody'/'bass'). By my own count of the 330 metadata files in the MedleyDB repo, 62 songs have two or more guitar stems and 34 of those have one tagged 'melody', which is only a weak stand-in for a lead label. The largest possible source of labels is DadaGP: 26,181 Guitar Pro songs, research access on request. Its paper says most songs have two distorted guitars, 'often one rhythmic and one lead'. However, its encoder groups guitars only by MIDI program (distorted0..2 / clean0..1) and never writes track names into the tokens. So lead/rhythm labels have to be inferred from the notes themselves: chords vs single notes, pitch register, bend and vibrato density, palm mutes. Realistic audio can be made by reamping: GOAT (ISMIR 2025) put its guitar recordings through about 7,000 public Neural Amp Modeler profiles from 1,000+ amps using Pedalboard and published the code. SynthTab already provides about 13,113 h of per-track renders of DadaGP (CC BY-NC, about 2 TB). Commercial prior art exists: Moises has offered lead/rhythm separation since April 2024 (no metrics published). MVSEP released 'MVSep Lead/Rhythm Guitar' on 2025-11-20, reporting SDR 9.21 for a two-stage version (guitar extractor, then a lead/rhythm model) and 9.02 for a one-stage 3-stem version. The test set is undisclosed and there is no public lead/rhythm leaderboard, so these numbers cannot be compared with anything. Community experience is consistent: it works when the lead is loud or panned to the centre with rhythm guitars on the sides (scenario A). It breaks when the two parts are balanced, share one tone and sit near the centre (B), when delay or reverb spreads across both channels, or when there are two rhythm or two lead parts. One hobbyist who trained Mel-RoFormer and Demucs lead/rhythm models with the ZFTurbo training framework (MSST) called his own result 'very mediocre'. The nearest academic result is GuitarDuets (two classical guitars, same timbre, trained with permutation-invariant 'guitar A / guitar B' outputs). On real recordings the first guitar reached 5.88 dB SDR but the second collapsed to 0.92 dB; ground-truth note labels fixed this, while estimated notes helped only marginally. That is a strong warning for scenarios B and F. By contrast, SpaIn-Net showed that telling the model where a source is panned can pull apart two guitars of the same kind (+1.8 dB SI-SDR in Slakh-based experiments). On compute: MSST supports custom stems, LoRA and loading a partly matching checkpoint. A small open starting point exists: becruily's Mel-RoFormer guitar model (dim 256, depth 4, 45 MB). The framework's default configs assume a 48 GB A6000, and the RTX 2060 has no bf16 and no official FlashAttention support. So the 2060 is for fp16 prototyping with short chunks, and a real run should use a rented 4090 or A6000 (about $0.33–0.34/h on RunPod community cloud). Verdict: do not train first. Start with MVSEP Lead/Rhythm plus the existing acoustic/electric and phantom-centre models, which already cover scenarios A, C and D. Train only a second-stage lead/rhythm model that takes the guitar stem as input, on reamped DadaGP/SynthTab mixtures with inferred labels, and only if a small real evaluation set shows MVSEP failing on your genres. Treat B, E and F as problems to fix in the note data after transcription, because audio models from every source reviewed here fail on them.

[RECOMMENDATIONS]
 - Before training anything, build a real evaluation set of 15–20 of your own target songs, tagged by scenario A–F. Run MVSep Lead/Rhythm (both variants), MVSep Electric/Acoustic Guitar, and the guitar-then-phantom-centre cascade on it. Score with a transcription-level metric, e.g. note F1 of Basic Pitch output on the separated lead against a reference tab, not only SDR or listening. Train only if these fail on the scenarios you care about.
 - If you train, copy MVSEP's winning design: a two-stage system where an existing guitar extractor feeds a 2-stem lead/rhythm model that only ever sees guitar-only input. This shrinks the domain, the data needs and the compute compared with a full-mix 3-stem model.
 - Warm-start from becruily's open Mel-RoFormer guitar checkpoint (dim 256, depth 4, ~45 MB) in MSST, using partial checkpoint loading or LoRA, Adam around 9e-6, fp16. Prototype on the RTX 2060 with chunk_size cut to about 3–6 s, batch 1 and gradient accumulation. Do the real run on a rented RTX 4090 or A6000 (~$0.33–0.34/h community) for 2–5 days, roughly $15–40. That cost figure is my estimate, not a benchmark.
 - For data, request DadaGP (research) and/or download SynthTab's per-track renders. Infer lead/rhythm per bar, not per track, from chord ratio, register, bend/vibrato and palm-mute density. Reamp with random NAM profiles and cabinet IRs using GOAT's public code. Explicitly simulate double-tracked rhythm (two independent takes, hard left/right), centred lead with delay/reverb, and 20–30% mono-summed examples so the model does not rely only on panning.
 - Randomize tone for both roles over the same pool of amps and effects. Otherwise the model learns 'lead = the renderer's high-gain preset' instead of the musical role, a shortcut that fails on real recordings.
 - Validate on real multitracks, never only on synthetic data. Candidates: the 34 MedleyDB songs that have two or more guitar stems including one tagged 'melody' (check the labels by hand), MoisesDB songs with two or more guitar tracks (label roles by hand), and optionally the MedleyDB frame-level lead annotations from Ou et al. 2025.
 - For B, E and F, plan for roles to be decided after transcription: separate into guitar A/B (or keep one guitar stem), transcribe, then assign lead/rhythm per segment in the note data, e.g. with a small classifier trained on DadaGP track pairs. Audio models from every source reviewed here are weakest in these cases.
 - Stay within non-commercial research licences (MoisesDB CC BY-NC-SA, SynthTab CC BY-NC, DadaGP and GOAT research-on-request), which fit personal use. Do not use isolated guitar tracks reached through a site's paywall loophole, even though a community guide suggests it.

[VERIFIED TAKEAWAYS]
 - No public audio dataset labels lead vs rhythm guitar. MoisesDB and MedleyDB have only timbre classes. In MedleyDB, only about 17 publicly released songs (v1+v2) have two or more guitar stems with exactly one tagged 'melody', not 34.
 - Symbolic role labels already exist. Régnier et al. (ISMIR 2021) published a bar-level rhythm-guitar classifier (F1 0.95 on 102 tabs), and D'Hooge et al. and Anoufa et al. ran it over DadaGP. The note-level role classifier the output proposed as new is prior art with released code and labels.
 - MVSep Lead/Rhythm Guitar (created 2025-11-20) exists: two-stage SDR 9.21, one-stage 9.02, test set undisclosed, no public leaderboard, weights not public. There are no public user reports on its per-scenario failure modes. The failure reports in the community guide are about the phantom-centre cascade.
 - Scenario D (acoustic rhythm plus electric lead) is covered by MVSEP's Acoustic Guitar (2025-08) and Electric Guitar (2025-10) models. Scenario C (clean electric plus distorted electric) is not covered by any off-the-shelf class model.
 - GuitarDuets shows that same-timbre two-guitar separation collapses on real data: G1 5.88 dB, G2 0.92 dB. Ground-truth-note conditioning was only shown to help on synthetic data. On real data, estimated notes did not fix G2 (1.01 to 1.04 dB).
 - SynthTab electric renders are DI only, only part of the electric tracks was rendered, and a note-dropping bug is documented. Building aligned lead+rhythm pairs will likely mean rendering from DadaGP yourself and reamping, for example with GOAT's render_amp.ipynb plus self-collected NAM profiles.
 - Compute: MSST uses PyTorch SDPA, so missing FlashAttention-2 on Turing or Windows is not a blocker; VRAM is. MSST's published batch tables start at 16 GB. On a 6 GB RTX 2060, expect about 2–3 s chunks at batch 1 for a dim-256 Mel-RoFormer (not verified). Rented RTX 4090 ($0.34/h) or A6000 ($0.33/h) on RunPod community cloud are confirmed prices.
 - The MSST paper (arXiv 2607.23395, July 2026) confirms custom stems, 7 dataset types, LoRA via --train_lora, partial loading via --start_check_point, and Adam at about 9e-6 for fine-tuning. becruily's open guitar Mel-RoFormer (dim 256, depth 4, 45 MB) exists but states no licence.

AREA: evaluation

[SUMMARY]
No public dataset labels real band-recording guitar stems as "lead" or "rhythm." Every multitrack corpus labels guitars by timbre or instrument instead. MoisesDB uses clean electric, distorted electric, lap steel/slide and acoustic. MedleyDB uses distorted electric, clean electric, acoustic and lap steel. Cambridge-MT file names follow the "NN_ElecGtrN[a/b][DI|Amp|DT]" pattern. That means you have to add the role labels yourself, or take them from a proxy. I parsed the MS21/MS500 annotation CSV, which covers 541 Cambridge-MT projects. 383 of those projects have electric guitar, 257 have two or more distinct numbered ElecGtr parts (101 in Rock/Punk/Metal), and only 5 have "Lead"/"Solo" in a guitar file name. Cambridge-MT is therefore the largest pool of real multi-guitar raw tracks, but you must label roles by ear. Its terms cover mixing practice and education, and they say research has not been specifically agreed with contributors. Personal, non-redistributed evaluation is the closest fit, but that is a judgment call; the page itself was behind Cloudflare, so I saw its terms only through search snippets. MedleyDB (CC BY-NC-SA, access by Zenodo request) has the best ready-made proxy. Each stem has a "component: melody" flag and per-stem activation curves. I parsed the public metadata: 18 public songs (all from v1, 12 of them Rock) have at least one guitar stem flagged melody and at least one that is not. That is almost exactly a 20-song lead/rhythm test set on real recordings, though "melody" is not the same as "lead" and should be checked by listening. For string/fret ground truth, real band mixes have none. Use GuitarSet, which records the same player, progression and tempo as a "comp" and a "solo" version with per-string annotations (360 excerpts, acoustic only). For electric guitar, use SynthTab (DadaGP tabs rendered per string, 23 timbres, multiple guitars per song). On metrics, SDR/SI-SDR only tells you about the signal (Le Roux et al. show SDR is easy to misuse). What matters for this tool is note-level F1 on the target role (mir_eval: 50 ms onset, 50 cents), a "role leakage" rate (rhythm notes that show up in the lead output), and tab F1/TDR (the TabCNN definitions). Two ceilings put these in context: Basic Pitch reaches onset-only note F1 (Fno) of 0.79 on clean isolated GuitarSet audio, and TabCNN reaches tab F 0.748 with TDR 0.899. Separation errors can only push you below those numbers. Practical verdict: build a hybrid set of about 20 items: roughly 12 MedleyDB v1 songs with a melody guitar vs other guitars, 4–6 Cambridge-MT rock songs you role-label by ear and remix into scenarios A/B/F, and 4–6 GuitarSet comp+solo duets or SynthTab duets for tab-level scoring. Use Basic Pitch run on the true isolated stem as the reference, so separation damage is measured apart from transcription error. Rank methods by lead-note F1 and leakage, not SDR. All of this runs locally on the Ryzen/RTX 2060 machine because the scoring is CPU work.

[RECOMMENDATIONS]
 - Make the primary metric target-role note F1 (mir_eval, 50 ms / 50 cents, report no-offset and with-offset), referenced to Basic Pitch run on the true isolated stem. Add role leakage (the share of predicted lead notes that match only rhythm GT) as the second headline number. Keep SDR/SI-SDR as a diagnostic column only.
 - Build a 20-item hybrid evaluation set: (1) about 10–12 of the 18 MedleyDB v1 songs with a melody-flagged guitar vs other guitars (prefer Rock: MusicDelta_Zeppelin, MusicDelta_SpeedMetal, Creepoid_OldTree, HopAlong_SisterCities, Meaxic_TakeAStep, TheScarletBrand_LesFleursDuMal, TheSoSoGlos_Emergency, BigTroubles_Phantom, TheDistricts_Vermont, MusicDelta_Shadows), after checking each role by ear; (2) 4–6 Cambridge-MT Rock/Punk/Metal projects you label by hand, each remixed into the A (hard-panned DT rhythm + centre lead), B (all centre) and F (mono) variants; (3) 4–6 synthetic duets with exact tab GT, from GuitarSet comp+solo or SynthTab electric renders.
 - Label roles per section, not per song. Use roughly bar or 4-second segments with labels {lead, rhythm, both, silent}, so scenario E (a lead that appears only in solos) can be scored separately and the evaluation does not reward a method that just outputs the loudest guitar.
 - Report results broken down by scenario (A–F), with per-song paired differences between methods and bootstrap confidence intervals. With 20 items, pooled averages hide the fact that panning-based tricks win on A and fail on B/F.
 - Include two cheap baselines every method must beat: (a) 'no separation', i.e. transcribe the whole guitar stem and call it lead, and (b) a symbolic post-hoc split such as a Régnier-style chord/single-note bar classifier, or a simple highest-voice or register rule. If an audio-domain lead/rhythm separator does not beat (b) on lead-note F1 and leakage, the symbolic route is cheaper.
 - Compute tab-level metrics (tab F1, TDR) only on the synthetic items that have string/fret GT. On real multitracks, run the same fretting algorithm on the oracle-stem notes and on the separated-stem notes and report string/fret agreement between the two.
 - Before using a ground-truth stem, check the bleed flags: MedleyDB has_bleed (PurlingHiss_Lolita = yes), and in MS21 85 of 2,477 electric guitar tracks are flagged, mostly drum-overhead bleed. Build Cambridge-MT references from the raw multitrack after your own mix, so the reference and the mixture are consistent.
 - Licensing: for this personal, non-commercial evaluation, keep MedleyDB/MoisesDB/Cambridge-MT audio local and never redistribute stems or trained models. Cambridge-MT's FAQ does not explicitly cover research, so treat it as educational/personal use and contact contributors if the tool ever becomes public or commercial.
 - Everything runs on the existing hardware. Scoring (Basic Pitch, mir_eval, museval) runs on CPU. The RTX 2060 is needed only to run the separators under test. No cloud GPU is justified for evaluation itself.
 - Analysis scripts from this research, for recomputing the dataset counts: <local-research>/ms\analyze.py, parts.py, tokens.py (Cambridge-MT/MS21), and ...\scratchpad\mdb\an.py, an2.py (MedleyDB).

[VERIFIED TAKEAWAYS]
 - The research was wrong that no lead/rhythm separator exists at MVSep. Since 2025-11-20 MVSep has offered 'MVSep Lead/Rhythm Guitar', and Moises has a 'Rhythm/Solo Guitars' target. Both should be baselines in the protocol. Their advertised SDRs (9.21 / 9.02) have no public lead/rhythm test set behind them, so only your own protocol can judge them.
 - Published same-instrument results show how hard this is: two electric guitar parts at -0.21 / 2.85 dB SDR (GCCE 2025), and classical guitar duets at 5.88 / 0.92 dB on real recordings (GuitarDuets, ISMIR 2024). By contrast, BS Roformer SW gets 9.05 dB for 'all guitar' on MVSep's 30-track guitar validation set.
 - Most dataset counts reproduce exactly: MS21 CSV 541 / 383 / 337 / 257 / 101 / 5 / 2; MedleyDB 18 songs, 12 Rock, all v1; MoisesDB 240 tracks and 14h24m46s; Basic Pitch 0.79 / 0.56 / 84.0; TabCNN 0.826 / 0.748 / 0.899; SynthTab 13,113 h, 20,715 tracks, 23 timbres; Régnier F1 0.95 vs 0.87.
 - Corrections to the evaluation design. (1) MedleyDB has human f0 ground truth for every melody guitar stem in the 18 songs, so use it instead of Basic-Pitch-on-oracle as the only reference. (2) The MusicDelta items are 17–36 s long. (3) The claim that v2 flags every guitar as melody is false; v2 has unflagged multi-guitar Rock songs to hand-label. (4) Basic Pitch was trained on GuitarSet, so GuitarSet duets are contaminated. (5) GuitarSet solos were recorded over the same player's own comping, so same-player pairs are natural duets.
 - For electric-guitar tab ground truth, add GOAT (ISMIR 2025, real DI plus reamped, tabs, parts split per guitar, research-only on request) alongside SynthTab (about 2 TB, CC BY-NC 4.0).
 - Cambridge-MT now lists 625 projects (July 2025). Its FAQ (confirmed via Wayback) calls research 'within the general spirit of educational use' but not agreed with contributors, and it has no AI clause. The MS500 audio was never released on Zenodo; download from Cambridge-MT directly and keep it local.
 - For scenario A, benchmark phantom-centre extraction (MVSep algorithm 54 / gilliaan models, with a public Phantom Center leaderboard) as a panning-only baseline alongside learned lead/rhythm models.
