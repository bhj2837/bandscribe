AREA: general-sota

[SUMMARY]
As of September 2026, the best openly downloadable 6-stem separator is BS-RoFormer "SW" (a BS-RoFormer checkpoint, dim 256, depth 12, about 700 MB). It outputs vocals, bass, drums, guitar, piano and other, and it tops the public MVSEP leaderboards. It scores guitar SDR 9.01 on MVSEP's 30-track guitar set and piano 7.80 on the 50-track piano set. On the Multisong set it scores bass 14.62, drums 14.11 and other 8.72. The older MVSep guitar models scored about 7.0 dB and Moises Guitar (2025) scored 6.94. HTDemucs htdemucs_6s, the usual default, is much weaker on the hard stems. Its own README says the piano stem "is not working great". On MoisesDB it scored only guitar 3.07 dB, piano 1.60 dB and other 0.28 dB, while bass (11.9) and drums (11.0) were solid. The Demucs upstream repo was archived in January 2025, and the maintainer says he is no longer actively working on it. Newer architectures push 4-stem results but add no guitar or keys stems: SCNet XL IHF (10.08 dB MUSDB average), BS-Conformer, BS-Mamba2, BS-PolarFormer, Moises-Light (9.96 dB with about 5M parameters per stem) and DTTNet. The main 2026 additions for "+@" are two. The MVSep Mega 53-stem BS-RoFormer (April 2026) has electric-guitar, acoustic-guitar, keys, synth, organ, strings and more, but the author recommends 16GB or more of VRAM and says its per-stem quality is below specialist models. The SJTU X-LANCE MSR-2025 checkpoints (MIT license, about 204 MB each) split SW's other/drums outputs further into synth, orchestral and percussion. No open model separates rhythm guitar from lead guitar. Only Moises offers this, as a proprietary feature with no published metrics, so that split has to be done after separation, using stereo placement and musical role. Community pipelines chain stages: vocals first, then instrumental = mix minus vocals, then drums and bass, then the residual. The MSST paper (July 2026) measures ensembling at only +0.01 to +0.2 dB and test-time augmentation at +0.00 to +0.06 dB, so a single SW pass captures almost all of the gain. Caveats: SW's provenance and license are unclear. Its Hugging Face license field is "unknown", its original host account was deleted (discovered 2026-06), and its leaderboard scores match "Logic Pro 11.2 Stem Splitter" to within about 0.01 dB. That is fine for personal use, but do not redistribute the weights. SDR also does not predict transcription usefulness well: a 2026 study found onset F-measure tracks SI-SDR only moderately (rho 0.62), and attack shape much less. Practical conclusion for this project: run BS-RoFormer-SW once locally through ZFTurbo's MSST inference.py or python-audio-separator, with fp16 autocast, batch 1, and a smaller chunk_size if it runs out of memory. Then add optional second stages: DrumSep on the drums stem, a phantom-centre or mid/side split on the guitar stem to pull lead from rhythm, and X-LANCE synth/orchestral models on the other stem. Judge every stage by Basic Pitch note-F1 on MoisesDB tracks, which have real guitar and piano stems, rather than by SDR. Runtime on the RTX 2060 6GB has not been measured by any source I found; it needs a local benchmark.

[RECOMMENDATIONS]
 - Use BS-RoFormer-SW as the single stage-1 separator (vocals/bass/drums/guitar/piano/other), run through MSST inference.py or `audio-separator -m BS-Roformer-SW.ckpt --use_autocast`. It beats htdemucs_6s by a wide margin on guitar and piano. Keep htdemucs_6s only as a fast baseline or fallback.
 - Fitting in 6GB VRAM: batch_size 1 and fp16 autocast (Turing has no native bf16). If it runs out of memory, lower config audio/inference chunk_size (e.g. 588800 to 352800) before anything else. Keep num_overlap 2. Skip TTA and ensembles at first; they add under 0.2 dB and multiply runtime. Benchmark one 4-min song yourself, since no public RTX 2060 timing exists.
 - Download the SW checkpoint once and keep a local copy with its SHA256. Hosting has already moved once (jarredou account deleted), and the license is 'unknown', so keep it personal and never bundle it in a public release.
 - Rhythm vs lead guitar: no open separation model exists, so split after separation. (a) Stereo: run mid/side or the MDX23C phantom-centre model on the guitar stem, because rhythm parts are often double-tracked and hard-panned while leads sit near the centre (heuristic, genre-dependent). (b) Musical role, after Basic Pitch: chordal/polyphonic, low-register, repeating material goes to rhythm; monophonic, bent or high-register lines go to lead. (c) Time: solo sections. Use Moises' paid lead/rhythm feature only as a reference to compare against.
 - Keys and '+@': SW's piano stem covers acoustic and electric piano. Synth pads, strings and FX mostly land in 'other'. Try X-LANCE syn_mss/orch_mss (MIT, about 204 MB) on SW's other stem. Treat MVSep Mega 53-stem as out of reach on 6GB (16GB or more recommended), and MVSEP's web-only Keys/Synth/Organ models as a manual fallback. Accept that 'other' stays a catch-all residual.
 - Drums and bass: SW's drums (14.1) and bass (14.6 Multisong) are already strong enough for transcription. Add DrumSep MDX23C on the drums stem only if you want per-piece drum notation.
 - Evaluate by downstream note accuracy, not SDR. Build a small test set from MoisesDB (real guitar/piano/keys stems), run Basic Pitch on the true stems and on the separated stems, and compare note-F1 and onset-F1. The 2026 'Beyond SDR' result shows SDR rankings do not predict transient fidelity.
 - Watch the classic confusions: distorted guitar vs synth lead or organ, and clean electric guitar vs electric piano. These show up as notes leaking between the guitar, piano and other stems. Plan a cleanup step downstream (e.g. drop notes that also appear strongly in another stem at the same time).

[VERIFIED TAKEAWAYS]
 - BS-RoFormer SW is the best downloadable 6-stem model on MVSEP's guitar board (9.01 SDR, 30 tracks, acoustic and electric) and piano board (7.80 SDR, 50 tracks). On Multisong it scores bass 14.62, drums 14.11 and other 8.72, but it is not #1 there: ensembles and several possibly non-public single entries score about 0.1-0.25 dB higher.
 - htdemucs_6s is far weaker on the hard stems (MoisesDB SDR: guitar 3.07, piano 1.60, other 0.28). It is still a valid fast MIT baseline, and Demucs got a maintenance release (v4.1.0) on 2026-07-11.
 - SW's license and provenance are unknown. The original author's HF and GitHub accounts are gone (404 on 2026-09-24). Working sources are enerjazzer/BS-ROFO-SW-Fixed (699 MB) and python-audio-separator's model-configs release. Keep a local copy with its SHA256 and do not redistribute it. The X-LANCE derivatives inherit this issue despite their MIT tag.
 - If you use python-audio-separator, use 0.47.0 or later. Older versions produced a wrong SW drums stem (before 0.39.1) or a different overlap schedule from MSST (before 0.47.0). MSST's own config already enables fp16 AMP for SW (use_amp: true).
 - SW fitting in 6GB and its runtime on an RTX 2060 are unmeasured. On Windows with sm_75, MSST uses math or memory-efficient attention instead of flash attention. Benchmark locally, and reduce chunk_size first if it runs out of memory.
 - No open-weights lead/rhythm guitar separator was found. Two hosted options exist: Moises (since 2024, app) and MVSEP (since 2026-03, website and API sep_type 101). The local route is still post-separation heuristics (stereo or phantom-centre, musical role, section timing), and none of them has a benchmark.
 - For keys, X-LANCE key_mss (bs_keys_xlancer 7.78 on the piano board) is effectively tied with SW's piano stem. For '+@' material, X-LANCE syn and orch models are available, but they were trained for restoration and have no published per-stem separation SDR. Mega 53-stem officially needs 16GB or more; its 6GB behavior is untested.
 - TTA adds at most about 0.06 dB. Ensembles add roughly 0.2-0.5 dB over the best single member (MSST Table 4; bass SW+SCNet XL goes from 14.62 to 14.87) at a multiple of the runtime. A single SW pass is a reasonable first stage but not 'almost all' of the attainable SDR.
 - SDR is a reasonable proxy for onset timing (Beyond SDR: onset F tracks SI-SDR, rho 0.62) but not for attack or dynamics shape, and no study links separation SDR to Basic Pitch note-F1 on guitar. Evaluate stages by transcription metrics on MoisesDB (non-commercial research license) or similar.

AREA: guitar-split

[SUMMARY]
As of 2026-09, separating guitars from the rest of the mix is a solved-enough problem you can run locally. Splitting one guitar from another is not. For all guitars together, the best open model is BS-Roformer SW (6 stems, 699 MB checkpoint). It scores about 9.0 dB SDR on MVSEP's 30-track guitar set, about the same as Apple Logic Pro 11.2 (9.00). It beats htdemucs_6s (5.22) and Moises Guitar 2025 (6.94). You can run it with python-audio-separator, UVR 5.6 or ZFTurbo MSST. I found exactly one lead/rhythm model with published numbers: 'MVSep Lead/Rhythm Guitar', created 2025-11-20. It has a two-stage variant (SDR 9.21) and a one-stage variant (SDR 9.02), but MVSEP does not say what data or metric those numbers come from, there is no public lead/rhythm leaderboard, and the weights are not published. You can only use it through mvsep.com or its paid-credit API. Moises (since April 2024) and the Music.AI 'Guitar Parts' API (rhythm, solo and other outputs) also split lead from rhythm, but they publish no metrics, and a community report says they fail when lead and rhythm are equally loud. LALAL.AI (unified Guitars stem, August 2026) and AudioShake (electric/acoustic, January 2026) do not split by role at all. On data, no public multitrack dataset labels guitars as lead or rhythm. MoisesDB's guitar sub-stems are acoustic, clean electric and distorted electric. MedleyDB only has a 'component: melody' flag. Research on separating two instances of the same instrument backs this up. Separating two classical guitars reaches only 4.95 and 1.01 dB SDR (GuitarDuets 2025). Query-by-example Banquet gets 0.9–1.7 dB median SNR on fine guitar sub-stems and its authors say it is insensitive to fine timbre differences. Meta's SAM Audio (December 2025) says separating 'highly similar audio events' is still a challenge and does not accept audio as a prompt. The approach that fits rock band covers is spatial. Rhythm guitars are usually double-tracked and panned hard left and right, so the two sides are uncorrelated. A solo lead is usually centered, so it is correlated between channels. Phantom-center extractors (gilliaan MDX23C/BS-Roformer: 18.93 and 16.45 center-SDR on MVSEP's phantom-center set) or a simple ILD/panning-index mask (Avendano 2003, ADRess 2004) applied to the guitar stem can pull those apart. Community reports say this works in rock tracks with a centered lead and fails with long or ping-pong delays, heavy reverb, two leads or two rhythms, and mono or live recordings. Practical conclusion: don't make lead/rhythm separation a hard requirement for your tab pipeline. Run BS-Roformer SW locally to get one all-guitar stem. Try a cheap spatial center/sides split when the guitar stem is actually wide. From double-tracked sides, transcribe only one side. Then decide lead vs rhythm at the note or segment level after Basic Pitch, using polyphony, register and density, as in Shibata et al. 2019's joint lead/bass/rhythm model. Use MVSep's lead/rhythm model through the API only as an optional second opinion.

[RECOMMENDATIONS]
 - Build the pipeline so lead/rhythm separation is optional: all-guitar stem, then transcription (Basic Pitch), then a role-assignment step. Only one benchmarked lead/rhythm model exists (MVSep, cloud-only, metric undefined), and same-timbre separation research is still at roughly 1–5 dB.
 - Stage 1, local: run python-audio-separator with BS-Roformer-SW.ckpt to get the guitar stem (about 9.0 dB SDR, best open model found). Benchmark VRAM and speed on the RTX 2060 first (lower chunk size or batch, try fp16).
 - Stage 2, spatial split on the guitar stem only: measure inter-channel correlation or width per segment. If the guitar stem is wide, apply a panning-index/ILD soft mask (or gilliaan's MDX23C phantom-center model) to get center (lead candidate) and sides (double-tracked rhythm). If it is near-mono, skip this step and send it to role assignment.
 - When sides look like a double-track (L and R play the same part), transcribe only one side for tab. A single take gives Basic Pitch fewer smeared or duplicated onsets than the summed stereo.
 - Stage 4, role assignment after transcription: mark polyphonic chord blocks or strums as rhythm, and monophonic high-register or dense single-note lines as lead (Shibata et al. 2019 FHSMM as the principled version). Also let the user mark solo sections in the UI; it is cheap and robust.
 - Use MVSep Lead/Rhythm Guitar (two-stage) through the API as a comparison and fallback on 5–10 of your cover songs. Judge it by note-level accuracy after transcription, not by its self-reported 9.21 SDR.
 - If acoustic vs electric matters (for example an acoustic strum under distorted guitar), try MVSep Acoustic/Electric Guitar models or the Mega-53 single-stem checkpoints. LALAL.AI's 2026 finding that type classification caused split-instrument errors suggests keeping a merged all-guitar fallback.
 - Build a small evaluation set from MoisesDB (non-commercial use is allowed): take songs with 2 or more guitar tracks, remix them with known panning (hard L/R rhythm, center lead, and also a same-pan case), and measure both SDR and Basic Pitch note F1. This tells you when to trust the spatial split.
 - Do not spend early effort on SAM Audio or Banquet for lead vs rhythm. Both document weakness on similar timbres, and SAM Audio is gated and heavy for 6 GB.
 - Do not use the '250+ lead/rhythm guitar pairs' source suggested in the community UVR guide. It depends on a Patreon access loophole, so it is not a legitimate dataset.

[VERIFIED TAKEAWAYS]
 - All-guitar stem: BS-Roformer SW (6 stems, 699 MB) is the top open entry on MVSEP's 30-track guitar set at 9.012 SDR (9.05 on the model page), tied with Logic Pro 11.2 (9.002) and well ahead of htdemucs_6s (5.22). It runs through python-audio-separator (BS-Roformer-SW.ckpt), bs-roformer-infer (MIT) or MSST. Its weight license is 'unknown'. If a clear license matters, the MIT-licensed xlance guitar model (8.60) is the best alternative.
 - MVSEP's lead/rhythm guitar model (created 2025-11-20; two-stage 9.21, one-stage 9.02) is real but cloud-only (sep_type 101). The SDR figures have no published dataset or metric definition, and MVSEP has no lead/rhythm leaderboard.
 - Open lead/rhythm checkpoints do exist (listra92 Mel-Band Roformer, 337 MB; DryPaint HTDemucs, 110 MB, MSST-only), but none has benchmark numbers and one author calls his 'very mediocre'. Treat them as experiments, not a solved stage.
 - Research on separating two parts of the same timbre is still weak. GuitarDuets gets about 5–5.9 dB for guitar 1 and ≤1.4 dB for guitar 2 on real duets. Banquet's fine guitar medians are 0.4–3.0 dB SNR on MoisesDB (distorted electric about 2.8, not 1.7). SAM Audio states similar sounds remain a challenge.
 - Spatial splitting of the guitar stem is the most practical lead/rhythm tool for studio rock mixes. The panning conventions and community reports are confirmed, and phantom-center extractors are strong on the generic center task (up to 20.26 center SDR for NN models, 17.01 for the free Bertom DSP plugin). It fails with delay or reverb, same-pan parts, and mono or live recordings. The top MDX23C's author says it can hallucinate frequencies, so check it by transcription accuracy.
 - Mega-53 single-stem checkpoints are about 78 MB each, not 1.27 GB. electric-guitar (about 8.2 SDR) and acoustic-guitar (about 5.0) should be practical on 6 GB, but that is unverified locally.
 - Shibata et al. 2019 still uses HPSS and vocal separation first and outputs rhythm guitar as 24-class chord labels. It supports assigning roles after transcription but does not produce tab-ready voicings.
 - Separation SDR is a weak proxy for downstream accuracy. The Bake-Off shows SDR diverging from perception on non-vocal stems, and Yousician found the residual 'other' stem slightly better than an isolated guitar stem for strum detection. Pick models by note or onset F1 on your own songs.

AREA: keys-synth-fx

[SUMMARY]
As of Sept 2026, keys are the weakest 'standard' stem. The best openly downloadable model is BS-Roformer-SW (6 stems: vocals, bass, drums, guitar, piano, other). On MVSEP's validation it scores 7.83 dB SDR for piano, against 2.23 dB for htdemucs_6s. Demucs' own README admits its piano stem has "a lot of bleeding and artifacts", so htdemucs_6s should only be a fallback. Beyond piano, the only open, local option that covers organ, synth, keys, digital piano, strings, brass and wind is ZFTurbo's MVSep Mega 53-stem BS-RoFormer, released 2026-04-20. The full 1.37 GB model recommends 16 GB or more of VRAM, its authors say it is weaker than MVSEP's specialized models, and its stems do not sum to the mix. A community split into 53 single-stem checkpoints (about 78 MB each, dim 256, depth 12) is the realistic way to try it on 6 GB, but that VRAM fit is my untested inference. MVSEP also hosts dedicated Keys, Organ, Synth, Rhodes, Clavinet, Mellotron, Bowed Strings, Brass, Woodwind, Wind and Risers models, but only on its site/API. It publishes no SDR for Keys, Synth or Organ, and their weights are not in the public MSST/UVR model lists. Query-based research models do not rescue the long tail. Banquet (query-by-example, MoisesDB) reaches only about 2.5 dB median SNR on piano, and for organs, synth pad/lead, string section, brass, reeds and FX it had "no sample performing above 5 dB SNR". Its authors found that extraction quality tracks how loud the target part is (Spearman rho 0.78-0.81), so quiet supporting parts like pads and FX stay poorly separated by nature. Meta's SAM Audio (Dec 2025; 500M/1B/3B; text, visual and time-span prompts) is the headline 2025-26 open-vocabulary model. Its 'professional instrument' win over Demucs, though, was measured only on 10 s MUSDB chunks for bass, drums and vocals, rated subjectively. It resamples to 48 kHz and downmixes to mono. On an A100, the Large model takes 7.3 s per 10 s of audio. One independent tester got silence for "keyboard" and "guitar" prompts, and a 4.5-minute song took over 5 hours on an M1 Max. The Small model has gone out of memory even on 16 GB unless you use a community reduced-precision, text-only fork (claimed 4-6 GB). The general text-queried models AudioSep, CLAPSep and FlowSep score far below dedicated music separators on instrument stems in SAM Audio's own comparison. Practical conclusion for the RTX 2060: separate in stages. Run BS-Roformer-SW for 6 stems first. Then run specialized single-stem models (Mega-53 split checkpoints, or MVSEP online for hard cases) on the 'other'/'piano' residual. Always keep a leftover track (mixture minus all extracted stems) and drop stems that come out near-silent. Use user-guided tools (a time-span or example-clip query via Banquet, or SAM Audio on short spans) only for one tricky part at a time. Expect keys at about 8 dB SDR at best, and treat pads and SFX as mostly not transcribable.

[RECOMMENDATIONS]
 - Use staged separation instead of one model. Stage 1: run BS-Roformer-SW locally for vocals, drums, bass, guitar, piano and other. Treat 'piano' as the keys stem; at 7.83 dB SDR it is the best open option, far ahead of htdemucs_6s at 2.23 dB.
 - Stage 2: run single-stem specialists on the Stage-1 'other' (and 'piano') output, not on the full mix. MVSEP reports better results from 'filtered sources'. Candidates are the 2026-04 Mega-53 single-stem split checkpoints for keys, organ, synth, strings/bowed_strings, brass and wind. Try batch 1, fp16 and chunk_size 441000 first on the 2060, and measure VRAM and speed yourself.
 - Always compute a leftover track (mixture minus every extracted stem, e.g. MSST --extract_instrumental) and route all unlabeled '+@' content (pads, FX, risers, samples) there. Do not try to tab SFX or pads; transcribe them only if the user explicitly asks.
 - Handle an unknown number of instruments by oversupplying candidate stems, then pruning near-silent ones. Use per-window RMS or energy thresholds and the ratio of stem energy to mix energy, since Mega-53's authors suggest filtering empty stems. Expect overlapping outputs, because stems do not sum to the mix.
 - For a part the fixed models miss, add a user-guided mode: the user marks a time span where that part plays (ideally alone) and it becomes (a) a Banquet same-song query or (b) a SAM Audio span anchor on a short window. GuideSep is an option if the user can play the line on a similar-sounding instrument. Keep these per-part and per-section; do not run SAM Audio over whole songs on a 2060.
 - Judge separation by downstream transcription accuracy, not SDR. Build a small personal test set from MoisesDB (it has piano, other keys, synth, strings and brass stems; non-commercial use is fine). Compare Basic Pitch note-F1 on separated versus ground-truth stems per instrument family.
 - Set expectations: even the best open keys model is about 8 dB SDR. Banquet showed quiet supporting parts (synth pads, organ beds, string sections) are hardest, with SNR tracking the part's loudness (rho about 0.8). Plan to transcribe only prominent keys/lead-synth lines and accept cleanup (pitch-range limits, removing short ghost notes).
 - Keep the MVSEP web models (Keys, Synth, Organ, Rhodes, etc.) as an optional online fallback for hard songs. Note this means uploading copyrighted audio to a third party, and Keys/Synth quality numbers are unpublished.

[VERIFIED TAKEAWAYS]
 - For piano and electric piano, the strongest downloadable model is BS-Roformer-SW. It scores about 7.8 dB SDR on MVSEP's 50-track piano set, versus 2.23 for htdemucs_6s. Three systems are statistically tied at the top: SW, Logic Pro 11.2 and bs_6stem. SW's architecture is small (dim 256, depth 12), so running it on 6 GB is plausible but unmeasured. Its license and provenance are unknown, possibly Logic-Pro-derived, which matters only if the tool is ever shared.
 - Keys beyond piano, plus organ, synth, strings and wind, can be handled locally with the MVSep Mega-53 single-stem split (about 78 MB each, dim 256, depth 12). Public or MVSEP-reported quality: piano 6.85 (leaderboard), wind 8.63 (leaderboard, second best overall), keys 9.3 / organ 10.4 / synth 2.05 / strings 8.8 (MVSEP card, unstated set). The full 53-stem model needs 16 GB or more and is not an option on a 2060.
 - Synth is effectively unsolved: 2.05 dB for Mega-53 synth, and Banquet never exceeded 5 dB on synth pad or synth lead. Bowed strings top out at about 5.4 dB on MVSEP's strings leaderboard. Pads, strings and FX should be treated as best-effort or leftover material, not tab targets.
 - Banquet's MoisesDB study shows that separation quality for long-tail stems correlates with the target's loudness (Spearman rho 0.78-0.81). Quiet supporting parts will separate worst whatever the model.
 - Separating in stages (main stems first, then specialists run on filtered stems) is MVSEP's own production design ('Ensemble All-In'), and MVSEP explicitly recommends Mega-53 as a detector for which stems exist, followed by specialist models. There is no independent ablation proving the gain.
 - A true leftover track (mixture minus all extracted stems) must be computed in your own code. MSST's --extract_instrumental only subtracts vocals, or the first stem, from the mixture.
 - SAM Audio is a poor fit for this user's machine. It requires Python 3.11 or newer (the user has 3.10). Its public repo has no long-form multi-diffusion. Small needs about 6 GB in bf16, which Turing only emulates, or about 10 GB in fp32. FP16 reportedly breaks its separation. Small is not better than Demucs on MUSDB bass/drums/vocals (4.24 vs 4.26 OVR). Music prompts such as 'keyboard' or 'guitar' returned silence in an independent test. At most, try it on short spans through a stripped-down fork.
 - The general text-queried separators AudioSep, CLAPSep and FlowSep score 2.0-2.6 OVR on MUSDB instrument prompts, against 4.26 for Demucs, so they are not viable for band stems. Among query-based models, Banquet (non-commercial weights) and GuideSep (needs a guide with matching timbre) suit only one-off, user-guided extraction of a single part.
 - No public keys, organ or synth leaderboard exists, and no published study links separation SDR to transcription F1 for keys. A local evaluation on MoisesDB (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), measuring Basic Pitch note-F1 on separated versus ground-truth stems, is the right way to choose models.

AREA: transcription-coupling

[SUMMARY]
Published evidence says to use separation to remove sources that separate cleanly (vocals, drums, bass). It should not be treated as a clean guitar extractor feeding a generic pitch tracker. Guitar stems from current separators are reported at "barely above 0 dB SNR". Vocals, bass and drums are reported at about 10 dB. The most directly relevant study transcribes rhythm-guitar strums in polyphonic music (Lukoianov & Klapuri, WASPAA 2025). There, the HTDemucs 4-stem residual "other" (mix minus vocals, drums and bass) beat the dedicated HTDemucs-6s guitar stem. With a frozen encoder, separation helped a lot: F1 78.6 on the full mix, 87.0 on the guitar stem, 88.2 on "other". After fine-tuning, the gap almost disappeared: 96.5 full mix vs 96.9 "other". YourMT3+ shows the same pattern for singing. A model without cross-stem augmentation scored 3.6 note F1 on raw mixes and 68 on Spleeter-separated vocals. The augmented YourMT3+ scored 71.1 on the mix vs 72.1 on separated input. So separation matters most when the downstream model was trained only on clean isolated audio, which is Basic Pitch's case. Robust or fine-tuned models gain little from it. Polyphony is still the main killer. In the 2025 AMT Challenge, the best system fell from 0.72 F1 on solo excerpts to 0.44 with three instruments, so thinning the mix does help. Distorted electric guitar is the hardest material even when fully isolated. On GOAT (2/3 distorted), off-the-shelf note F1 was: Basic Pitch 0.553, MT3 0.291, MuScriptor-medium 0.710. The best retrained models reach about 0.75. Clean electric (EGDB) is easier: Basic Pitch 0.718, Harmonica 0.881. Direct transcription from real mixes has improved a lot with MuScriptor (Kyutai/Mirelo, ISMIR 2026, trained on 11k hours of real music). It reaches 60.4 onset F1 on real multi-instrument songs and 46.1 on RWC-Pop, vs 36.1 for YourMT3+. That is still far from tab-grade, and YourMT3+ scored under 10% on non-core instruments, guitar included, in commercial pop. For drums, separation is neutral to marginal. Demucs-v4-then-ADTOF matches ADTOF on the mix within about 2 points. Drum-kit stem separation mainly adds crash/ride classes and velocities. Tab-specific models (TabCNN, FretNet, Noise2Fret, TART) are trained and evaluated on isolated guitar only. TART's end-to-end tab F1 is about 54% even on isolated audio. Noise2Fret shows fret assignment is nearly solved once pitch is right (TDR about 0.99). The bottleneck is note detection, not string/fret choice. Practical conclusion: build a hybrid. (1) Separate vocals, drums and bass with a strong separator. (2) Transcribe drums with ADTOF and bass from the bass stem. (3) Run an instrument-conditioned, mixture-trained transcriber (MuScriptor, with distorted/clean e-guitar conditioning) on the "other" residual and/or the full mix. (4) Use a dedicated guitar stem only to route and verify which notes are guitar. (5) Do audio- or playability-aware fret assignment. (6) Expect manual correction. The biggest accuracy lever for a personal project is fine-tuning the guitar transcriber on the exact separator output it will see, with amp/distortion augmentation (e.g. GOAT, SynthTab). Upgrading the separator alone is not that lever.

[RECOMMENDATIONS]
 - Don't build 'best guitar stem, then Basic Pitch, then fret assignment' as the core. Basic Pitch is trained on clean isolated audio, reaches only 0.553 note F1 even on perfectly isolated distorted guitar (GOAT), and separated guitar is about 0 dB SNR. Use separation subtractively: remove vocals, drums and bass with a strong separator and keep the residual 'other' (guitars, keys, synths) as the guitar-transcription input. In the strum study this beat a dedicated guitar stem.
 - Use a mixture-trained, instrument-conditioned transcriber for guitars and keys. MuScriptor is the current best open option: it has distorted and clean e-guitar classes, exports tab, and its non-commercial weights license is fine for personal use. Run it on both the 'other' residual and the full mix, then compare or merge. Condition it on the known instrumentation of the cover song, which measurably helps.
 - Use a dedicated guitar-stem separator (or the keys/synth stems) for routing and verification, not as the only input. Example: accept a transcribed note as guitar only if the guitar stem has energy at that pitch and time; otherwise route it to keys or synth. This limits MT3-style instrument leakage and hallucinated notes.
 - Drums: run ADTOF on the Demucs drum stem or directly on the mix (published results are within about 2 points either way). Add the MDX23C DrumSep kit stems for crash/ride split and velocities (Riley & Dixon recipe). Drums are the part where separation is least critical.
 - Bass: separate the bass stem (bass separates at about 10 dB, far better than guitar) and transcribe it with a mostly-monophonic approach or MuScriptor with bass conditioning. This is where separate-then-transcribe works best. This is an inference from the reported separation SNRs, not a published tab-level benchmark.
 - Rhythm vs lead guitar: no transcription model in the literature separates two electric guitars of the same timbre class. Plan to split them after transcription, using symbolic cues (chord density, register, single-note lines) and stereo panning (L/R or mid/side of the residual). Treat this as a design hypothesis to test, not established practice.
 - The biggest measured lever is training on what you infer on. Fine-tuning closed a 78.6 to 96.5 gap on full mixes for strums. Cross-stem augmentation took vocals from 3.6 to 71 without separation. FX augmentation added about 10 points on electric guitar. If you train or fine-tune a guitar model, render GOAT or SynthTab tabs through amp sims, mix them with backing tracks, pass them through your exact separator, and train on that output so the model learns the separator's artifacts.
 - For string/fret assignment, prefer audio-conditioned or playability-aware models (Noise2Fret-style, TART stage 3) over pure MIDI-to-tab dynamic programming. Once pitch is correct, fret disambiguation is about 99% (Noise2Fret TDR), so pitch/onset detection is the bottleneck to invest in.
 - Set expectations and build the UI accordingly. Even isolated electric guitar tops out around 0.75 note F1 (distorted) and about 54% end-to-end tab F1 (TART), and real band mixes are harder. Build the tool as assistive: editable Guitar Pro/MusicXML output, A/B playback against the stem, and per-note confidence highlighting.
 - Build a small personal evaluation set before committing to a separator. Mix GOAT (or your own DI recordings with known tabs) with backing tracks, then compute mir_eval note F1 for three inputs: oracle isolated, separated stem, and full mix. No published paper measures guitar-tab accuracy on separated stems from real band mixes, so this directly answers whether a given separator is good enough for transcription.

[VERIFIED TAKEAWAYS]
 - Separation matters most when the downstream model was trained only on clean, isolated audio. Base YMT3 singing went from 3.62 on the mix to 67.98 on separated vocals, and frozen-MERT strums from 78.6 to 88.2. Mixture-trained or fine-tuned models gain little: YourMT3+ 71.07 vs 72.05, fine-tuned strums 96.5 vs 96.9. Basic Pitch is in the clean-only category, and its README says it works best on one instrument at a time.
 - The premise that guitar stems are about 0 dB is outdated. Current 6-stem separators reach about 9 dB guitar SDR on MVSep's guitar benchmark (BS Roformer SW 9.01, run locally via python-audio-separator or UVR 5.6), vs 5.25 for htdemucs_6s. The published evidence that the residual beats the guitar stem used htdemucs_6s on acoustic-guitar strum onsets, so it has to be re-tested before it drives the architecture.
 - Distorted electric guitar is hard even fully isolated. On GOAT, note onset+pitch F1 is Basic Pitch 0.553, MuScriptor-medium 0.710 off-the-shelf, and at best about 0.75 with retrained models. On mostly clean EGDB the best is 0.881.
 - MuScriptor is the strongest open mixture-trained multi-instrument transcriber with separate classes for clean and distorted electric guitar. Its weights are CC BY-NC (fine for personal use). It publishes no per-instrument accuracy. It always merges same-tone rhythm and lead guitars into one track, and its tab is MuseScore's automatic fingering. Medium fits a 6 GB RTX 2060; large needs `--dtype float16`, and on Windows `--torch-backend=cu128`.
 - Drums are where separation matters least. ADTOF on Demucs drums is within about 2 points of ADTOF on the mix (MDB 0.89 vs 0.87). DrumSep kit stems mainly add crash/ride and open/closed hi-hat classes plus velocities (MDB 8-class F 0.84 vs 0.72). Diffusion kit separation helps kick and snare but hurts hi-hats, cymbals and toms.
 - String/fret assignment is not solved in general. Noise2Fret's TDR of 0.99 holds only for correctly detected pitches on in-domain data, while TART's string-fret F1 with oracle MIDI is 71.8%, and its end-to-end tab F1 is 54.08% on isolated or noisy guitar.
 - No published study measures guitar note or tab F1 on separated stems from real band mixes against isolated ground truth. The only real-band per-instrument number found is STRUM's lenient guitar onset F1 of 0.651 (±100 ms, htdemucs_6s). A personal evaluation set (e.g., GOAT or your own DI recordings mixed with backing tracks, scored for oracle vs separated vs full mix) remains the right way to choose a separator.
 - No transcription or separation model splits rhythm and lead guitar of the same tone in band music. The nearest published work is classical guitar-duet separation (GuitarDuets), so any rhythm/lead split has to be designed and tested by you, e.g. from stereo panning or symbolic cues.
====================================================================================================
AREA: nonmodel-and-services

[SUMMARY]
As of Sept 2026, three commercial or semi-commercial options can split a full band mix into LEAD vs RHYTHM guitar. (1) Moises: its lead/rhythm and acoustic/electric guitar models date from April 2024, and its app now lists 27 stem types, including kick/snare/toms/hi-hat/cymbals, keys, piano, strings, wind and brass. (2) Music.AI, Moises's developer platform: a REST API with Python and Node SDKs, charging $0.07/min per basic stem and $0.10/min for 'Guitar parts (rhythm/solo)'. (3) MVSEP: added 'MVSep Lead/Rhythm Guitar' on 2026-03-21, reachable from a script through its API (sep_type 101, outputs lead-guitar and rhythm-guitar). Registered free users are limited to 1 concurrent job and MP3 output; premium is prepaid credits. LALAL.AI (electric/acoustic guitar, piano, synth, strings, wind; API only on the $15/mo Pro plan) and AudioShake (guitar, piano, strings, winds; enterprise API with no public price) do not advertise lead/rhythm splitting. Fadr and Serato give only coarse stems (vocals/drums/bass/'melody'), so they are useless for separating guitar parts. No public benchmark scores lead-vs-rhythm quality for any service. Even whole-guitar separation is much harder than bass or drums: the best guitar SDR on MVSEP's guitar leaderboard is about 9.0 dB (BS-Roformer SW, and Apple's Logic Pro 11.2 splitter at 9.00), while MVSEP's bass and drum models are reported at about 14–15 dB. Treat role-split guitar stems as good enough for guidance, not clean. The best non-model route is Karaoke-Version 'Custom Backing Tracks': $2.99/song, studio RE-RECORDINGS (not the original master) with up to about 15 separate tracks, often including separate 'Lead Electric Guitar' and 'Rhythm Electric Guitar'. After purchase you can download any number of mixes, so soloing one track at a time yields near-clean isolated MP3s. The arrangement is a reproduction, and coverage of Korean or Japanese band songs is unverified. Official artist stems (Beatport/NI Stems) are 4-stem and mostly electronic, so they rarely help band covers. For the workflow, practitioners combine separation with slow-down/loop tools (Transcribe!, Moises speed changer and chord detection) and check against community tabs (Songsterr, which since 2025–2026 also offers AI tab generation from YouTube or uploads, 50 per month on Plus). Songsterr AI and Klangio Guitar2Tabs work as ready-made end-to-end baselines the user's own pipeline should beat or match. For evaluation, no public dataset labels lead vs rhythm guitar. Slakh2100 (CC BY 4.0, synthesized, per-stem MIDI) is best for scoring separation plus transcription end to end. MoisesDB (240 real tracks, CC BY-NC-SA / non-commercial research) splits guitar into acoustic/clean electric/distorted electric. MUSDB18-HQ is only 4-stem and academic-only. GuitarSet (CC BY 4.0) has string/fret annotations for testing fret assignment. Cambridge-MT's 500+ free raw multitracks let you build your own lead/rhythm ground truth. On legality, Korean Copyright Act Art. 30 allows non-commercial private copying within a household-like circle. Service ToS (e.g., Moises) are aimed at personal use and put rights clearance on the user. YouTube's ToS prohibit downloading content without permission, and redistributing separated stems is not covered by any of these. Key conclusion: use services only as a quality ceiling or reference, build the pipeline so the separation backend can be swapped (local model / MVSEP API / Music.AI API), prefer Karaoke-Version multitracks whenever the song exists there, and evaluate on Slakh2100 + MoisesDB + self-built Cambridge-MT lead/rhythm sets.

[RECOMMENDATIONS]
 - Make the separation stage a swappable backend (local MSST/UVR model | MVSEP API | Music.AI API) behind one interface. Always cache stems to disk so paid runs happen once per song.
 - For the lead-vs-rhythm split, run a 2-stage cascade: (a) full mix → guitar stem with the strongest whole-guitar model (BS-Roformer-SW class, about 9 dB SDR); (b) guitar stem → lead/rhythm with MVSEP sep_type 101 or Music.AI 'Guitar parts (rhythm/solo)'. Whether these services expect a full mix or a pre-separated guitar is undocumented, so test both.
 - Add a cheap non-ML heuristic before or after role splitting. Rock mixes often double-track rhythm guitars hard L/R with the lead near centre, so compute mid/side or per-channel versions of the separated guitar stem and keep whichever gives cleaner pitch tracks. This is a general mixing convention, not a sourced claim; verify per song.
 - Before separating anything, check whether the song exists on Karaoke-Version Custom Backing Tracks ($2.99). If it does, download each instrument soloed. You get truly isolated lead and rhythm guitar (re-recorded) and skip separation entirely for that song.
 - Use the Moises app (manual) or Music.AI/MVSEP (scripted) outputs as a quality ceiling for your local models. Use Songsterr AI tabs or Klangio as an end-to-end baseline your tab output should match or beat.
 - Evaluation set: Slakh2100 (CC BY 4.0, synthesized, per-stem MIDI) for separation + transcription F1. MoisesDB (non-commercial research) for real-recording guitar/keys/synth SDR. GuitarSet (CC BY 4.0) for fret-assignment accuracy. Build 10–20 of your own lead/rhythm ground-truth mixes by summing Cambridge-MT raw multitracks. Also use MVSEP's downloadable Guitar and Multisong sets, which include K-pop, to compare against the public leaderboard.
 - Personal-use hygiene: prefer audio you legitimately own (CD/purchased files) over YouTube rips, whose ToS prohibit downloading. Keep stems and tabs private. Do not publish separated stems. Publishing tabs or performance covers is a separate licensing question.
 - Budget sanity check: at Music.AI list prices, a 4-minute song with 5 basic stems plus a rhythm/solo guitar split costs roughly $1.80. MVSEP's free tier works for experimentation but is limited to MP3 output and 1 concurrent job.

[VERIFIED TAKEAWAYS]
 - Only three verified services split guitar by role. The Moises app (Rhythm/Solo), the Music.AI API ('Guitar parts (Rhythm guitars, Solo guitars)', $0.10/min) and MVSEP (sep_type 101, lead-guitar and rhythm-guitar, added 2026-03-21) do. LALAL.AI, AudioShake, Fadr and Serato do not.
 - MVSEP self-reports lead/rhythm SDR of 9.21 (two-stage, default) and 9.02 (one-stage). The dataset is not stated and nobody has benchmarked it independently, so the user must measure it on their own ground truth.
 - Acoustic/electric guitar sub-stems are available from Moises/Music.AI, LALAL.AI, the AudioShake API and Fadr Plus. LALAL.AI Pro's offline 'Lyra' model does this locally on Windows.
 - Whole-guitar SDR tops out at about 9.0 dB on MVSEP's 30-track guitar set (BS Roformer SW 9.012, Logic Pro 11.2 9.002). Moises Guitar (2025) scores only 6.94 there. Commercial services are not an automatic quality ceiling; open models match or beat them on whole-guitar.
 - On MVSEP's own sets, bass (14.87) and drums (14.35) score far higher than guitar (9.05) and piano (7.83). The sets differ, though: Multisong for bass and drums, dedicated sets for guitar and piano.
 - Music.AI list-price cost for a 4-minute song is 5 basic stems × $0.07 × 4 = $1.40, plus rhythm/solo at $0.10 × 4 = $0.40, for $1.80. MVSEP's free registered tier gives MP3 only, 1 concurrent job, 50 separations a day, 10-minute files and limited API.
 - Karaoke-Version Custom Backing Tracks are confirmed: $2.99 per song, up to 15 re-recorded (not original) tracks, MP3 only, each isolated track downloaded one at a time by muting the others, pitch ±2 semitones. Coverage of Korean bands appears thin (low confidence).
 - Evaluation data: Slakh2100 (CC BY 4.0, synthesized, aligned MIDI; use the redux split). MoisesDB (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, non-commercial research, guitar labelled acoustic/clean/distorted, no lead/rhythm). MUSDB18-HQ (4 stems, academic only). GuitarSet (CC BY 4.0, string/fret labels, comp/solo pairs that can be summed into an acoustic lead/rhythm test set). MVSEP's guitar set only scores server-side.
 - Legal: Korean Copyright Act Art. 30 allows non-commercial private copying. A 2008 Seoul court ruling (2008카합968) excludes copying from sources known to be infringing, and YouTube's ToS forbid downloads without authorization. Use legitimately obtained audio and keep stems private.
 - There is no verified local lead/rhythm checkpoint. ZFTurbo MSST's pretrained list (checked 2026-09-24) has none, so for now lead/rhythm splitting means a cloud call (MVSEP or Music.AI) or the user's own heuristic or trained model.
